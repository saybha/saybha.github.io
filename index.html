Sayak Bhattacharjee

I am a theoretical physicist. Some of the most surprising phenomena in nature arise from macroscopic manifestations of the laws of quantum mechanics. I spend most of my time thinking about such problems.

I am a graduate student at Stanford University, and am advised by Srinivas Raghu.

The physics of condensed matter is the physics of the many. Properties of matter emerge from the collective behavior of a large number of constituent particles and can differ from those of the individual constituents. A copper wire conducts electricity but individual copper atoms cannot. Ocean water is turbulent, while a water molecule is benign. Simply put, I study phases of matter and the transitions that separate them.

This way of thinking is described succinctly in Phil Anderson's essay More Is Different (1972), and more luxuriously in Bob Laughlin's book A Different Universe: Reinventing Physics from the Bottom Down (2005).
